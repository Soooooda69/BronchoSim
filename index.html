Abstract

Learning-based bronchoscopic navigation needs large amounts of video with exact geometry, yet clinical recordings are scarce, lack reliable per-frame pose, and rarely capture the failure modes that matter most. Simulators provide perfect labels but look nothing like a real airway.

BronchoSim turns patient CT into photorealistic, breathing, fully annotated bronchoscopy video. For each patient we extract the airway mesh and centerline tree, simulate respiratory motion with a learned 4D-CT deformation model driven by real breathing traces, and plan camera paths through the bronchial tree, including navigation corner cases such as lateral wander, retract-and-readvance, wrong-branch recovery and lost-view recovery. A speed model learned from EM-tracked clinical procedures re-times each path. We render the deforming airway along every path, and a depth-conditioned video diffusion model, adapted to real bronchoscopy, turns the render into realistic endoscopic video while keeping its geometry. Every frame is paired with its 6-DoF camera pose and rendered metric depth.

How BronchoSim works

We keep geometry, motion and appearance separate. Geometry and motion come from patient anatomy and clinical kinematics, so the labels are exact by construction. Only the appearance is generated, and the generator is conditioned on the rendered geometry.

Geometry

1
Patient airway. The airway surface mesh, a signed-distance field and the branch-labelled centerline tree are extracted from each patient's CT.
2
Respiratory motion. A 4D-CT motion model (SeqX2Y) predicts phase-wise deformation fields from the CT, driven by real respiratory traces. We warp the mesh vertices over several breathing cycles and sample independent realizations per patient.

Motion

3
Path planning. Random start and goal branches are connected through the bronchial tree. The path is splined with SDF-based collision smoothing, off-centerline perturbation and inserted stops.
4
Learned pacing. A temporal CNN trained on EM-tracked clinical procedures predicts per-frame speed from visual features and local airway geometry, then re-times the path.
5
Corner cases. Short clips are synthesized for four families of navigation corner cases. Each frame is labelled forward / retract / left / right / up / down / stop.

Appearance

6
Render. The camera path is carried along with the tissue motion, and the deforming mesh is rendered at 30 fps with metric depth.
7
Depth-controlled video diffusion. We start from Cosmos-Transfer 2.5 (2B) with the Cosmos-H-Surgical depth weights, then fine-tune a depth-control branch and a rank-32 DiT LoRA on 275 real clinical bronchoscopy clips. Depth-Anything-V2 depth of the render drives generation, and long paths are generated in overlapping 81-frame windows. 14 appearance prompts (mucus, blood-tinged secretions, inflamed mucosa, light tint and others) diversify the look.

Diversity

BronchoSim covers the variety of real airways through patient-specific anatomy, and the variety of their appearance through prompt-controlled generation.

Each clip below comes from a different patient and was generated with a different appearance prompt. The prompt mainly sets tissue tone and lighting, from pale to inflamed mucosa and from cool to warm scope light, and can add sparse details such as blood or mucus. Fine texture follows the appearance learned from real bronchoscopy.

Coverage of real appearance (recall, k = 3; the share of real frames that fall inside the generated-frame manifold): raw render 0.00, BronchoSim 0.18, and held-out real 0.44. Recall is computed on InceptionV3 frame features of randomly drawn clips, compared against real clinical clips. The render covers none of the real appearance, and BronchoSim covers a substantial part of it, though less than real video.

Navigation corner cases

Clinical recordings mostly show smooth forward driving. Navigation policies fail in the rare moments between. BronchoSim synthesizes those moments on purpose. There are four families, each planned directly on the centerline tree and labelled per frame.

Lateral wander

The scope drifts off the centerline toward the airway wall while it advances, then corrects.

RenderBronchoSim

Retract & re-advance

The scope advances, backs out while still looking distally, and then advances to the same target again. In some clips it retreats all the way.

RenderBronchoSim

Revisit bifurcation / wrong branch

The scope enters one child branch, returns to the carina, and takes another. In asymmetric clips it goes shallowly into the wrong branch and then fully into the correct one.

RenderBronchoSim

Lost & recover

A large, sudden pose perturbation at a random moment points the scope at the wall. A smooth correction arc then brings it back to the lumen.

RenderBronchoSim

Autonomous navigation

Select a target branch on the airway map. A route is planned through the bronchial tree, and a learned navigation policy then drives the scope to the target on its own. The map shows the remaining route (grey), the path driven so far (blue) and the scope's current position (orange). Next to it are the live endoscopic view and the motion command and speed the policy issues at every step. Each video shows two runs to different targets.

What every sample contains

  • Generated video, 480×480, frame-aligned with the render
  • Geometric render of the deforming airway, 200×200 at 30 fps
  • 6-DoF camera pose per frame, both planned and breathing-deformed
  • Metric depth rendered from the deformed mesh
  • Airway context per frame: branch id, parent branch and airway generation
  • Motion label per frame (forward / retract / turn / stop) and corner-case category for corner-case clips
